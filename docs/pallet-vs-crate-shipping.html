Pallet and Crate Shipping: What's the Difference?

By Las Vegas Pack and Ship | 9 February, 2026

Pallet vs. Crate Shipping: Key Comparison

In logistics and freight shipping, pallets and crates serve different purposes. Pallets provide a base platform for stacking and moving goods (often with boxes or items secured on top), while crates are fully enclosed wooden or sometimes plastic/metal boxes that surround and protect the load on all sides. Pallets are the industry standard for efficient, high-volume shipping; crates excel when maximum protection is needed.

Here's a concise side-by-side comparison:

AspectPallets (e.g., Wooden Standard 48″x40″)Crates (Wooden or Custom Enclosed)
DesignOpen platform with top/bottom decks; items stacked on top and often shrink-wrappedFully enclosed box with walls, lid, and base; load secured inside
Protection LevelModerate – exposed sides; relies on wrapping/boxes for securitySuperior – full enclosure shields from impact, moisture, dust, theft
Best ForUniform, stackable, non-fragile goods (boxed products, bulk items, retail distribution)Fragile, high-value, oversized, irregular, or sensitive items (machinery, electronics, art, medical devices)
CostLower upfront and shipping (less material, lighter)Higher (more material, labor; often custom-built; heavier → higher freight)
Weight & Shipping FeesLighter overall; better for dimensional/weight-based ratesHeavier and bulkier; increases costs, especially international
Handling EfficiencyExcellent – forklift/jack compatible from all sides; fast loading/unloading; racking-friendlySlower; often requires more handling; less compatible with automation
StackabilityHigh when properly secured; stable in warehouses/trucksGood if designed for it, but enclosed design limits visibility/stability in some cases
ReusabilityOften reusable/recyclable; returnable pools commonReusable in some cases, but custom crates frequently one-way
Environmental ImpactGenerally sustainable (wood recyclable); less materialMore material used; higher footprint unless reused heavily
Typical Use CasesWarehouse storage, LTL/FTL trucking, domestic distribution, palletized containersInternational/export, heavy machinery, valuables, long-haul where damage risk is high

Bottom line

Choose wood pallets for speed, cost savings, and efficiency in most standard logistics scenarios (especially warehouses, e-commerce fulfillment, or domestic freight). Opt for crates when product protection outweighs cost and handling speed—such as for valuable, delicate, or export shipments. Many operations combine both (e.g., crated items palletized for easier transport). The right choice depends on your cargo type, value, fragility, and shipping distance.

Here's a more concise side-by-side comparison of crates (reusable plastic or wooden) vs. cardboard boxes for shipping and storage:

AspectCrates (Reusable Plastic / Wooden)Cardboard Boxes
DurabilityHigh – resists crushing, moisture, pests, repeated useMedium-low – weakens when wet or overloaded
Product ProtectionExcellent for heavy, fragile, or valuable itemsAdequate for light, non-fragile goods
StackabilitySuperior – rigid, interlocking, stable high stacksLimited – risks collapse under weight
Reusability100s of cycles (especially rental/pooling systems)Single-use or very limited reuse
Upfront CostHigher (purchase or rental fee)Very low or free
Long-Term CostLower – savings on damage, waste, repurchasingHigher – constant replacement + disposal
Environmental ImpactBetter over many cycles (less waste)Recyclable but high single-use volume
Handling EfficiencyFaster – no assembly, forklift-ready, nestable when emptyRequires folding, taping, slower packing
Best ForSupply chains, repeated shipments, long-term storage, heavy/valuable goodsOne-off, lightweight, low-budget moves

Bottom line:

Crates win for reliability, efficiency, and total cost in professional or repeated-use scenarios (warehouses, logistics, moving businesses). Boxes remain convenient for occasional, light-duty needs.

Pallets vs. Skids: Key Comparison in Shipping & Logistics

In warehousing, shipping, and material handling, pallets and skids are often confused or used interchangeably, but they differ mainly in design. Pallets are the modern standard for most logistics, while skids (the original “pallet” design) suit specific niche uses. The core difference: pallets have both a top and bottom deck (full flat surfaces top and bottom), while skids have only a top deck resting on runners/stringers (no bottom deck).

Here's a concise side-by-side comparison:

AspectPallets (Standard, e.g., 48″x40″ wooden)Skids (Single-Deck Platforms)
DesignTop deck + bottom deck + stringers/blocksTop deck only + runners/stringers (no bottom deck)
Stability & StrengthHigh – even weight distribution, excellent for stackingModerate – good for heavy bases but less even distribution
Forklift Access4-way entry (most designs); easy from all sidesOften 2-way entry; limited access
MobilityOptimized for forklifts, pallet jacks, racking systemsEasier to drag/slide (less friction); harder for stacking
CostHigher (more materials)Lower/cheaper (simpler construction, less wood)
Weight CapacityHigher (typically 2,000–5,000+ lbs dynamic)Adequate for heavy loads but less versatile
StackabilitySuperior – stable in high racks/warehousesLimited – not ideal for multi-level stacking
ReusabilityHigh – durable, returnable pools commonModerate – often one-way or stationary
Best ForShipping, transport, warehousing, distribution, repeated handlingStationary storage, heavy machinery bases, dragging heavy items, one-time/low-mobility uses
Typical Use CasesRetail/e-commerce fulfillment, LTL/FTL freight, palletized loadsIndustrial equipment foundations, temporary heavy storage, older facilities

Bottom line

Choose pallets for efficient, modern logistics—shipping, stacking in warehouses, and high-volume handling (the industry default for good reason). Go with skids when you need a simpler, cheaper, drag-friendly base for stationary or heavy/industrial applications where full forklift versatility isn't required. Many operations use pallets for transport and skids for in-house heavy-duty support.
